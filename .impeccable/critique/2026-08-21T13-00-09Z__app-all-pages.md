## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: The visual system is explicitly a borrowed template, not an authored one — `app/globals.css` even comments "Notion-style white canvas." The ink-black primary, hairline borders, indigo accent, Geist font, and Material Symbols icon set are a close copy of a generic productivity-SaaS look with no Mozambican visual signal. Data-layer localization is genuinely done well (MT currency, dd/MM/yyyy, Portuguese copy, a working "Dinheiro Móvel" account type), but nothing *visual* distinguishes a mobile-money account from a bank or cash account — same neutral card, same generic 16-color rainbow swatch picker. This reads as a well-built generic finance dashboard translated into Portuguese, not a product designed around Mozambique's specific account mix — which undercuts Product Principle #1 ("local fit over feature parity") on sight.

**Deterministic scan**: The static CLI detector (regex scan over all `.tsx`/`.css` source) came back clean — 0 findings, exit code 0 — verified against a synthetic test file to confirm the scanner itself isn't silently broken. The live in-browser overlay (which catches computed-style issues static scanning can't) found real, independent issues on 3 sampled pages: `cramped-padding` on Settings' password form (mobile), a `gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow` combination on the Dashboard's cards (a well-known "AI-generated look" tell), a skipped heading level (`h1`→`h3`, no `h2`) on Dashboard, and an `overused-font` advisory (Geist carrying 59-64% of text) on every page checked. That last one is arguably *not* a real issue here: `operate.md`'s own Typography guidance says "One family is often right... a well-tuned sans carries headings, buttons, labels, body, data" for Operate-mode product UI — so I'm treating that specific advisory as expected-for-mode rather than a defect.

**Visual overlays**: Screenshot capture failed in this session (the Browser pane wasn't compositing frames), so overlays aren't visible in a `[Human]` tab — Assessment B substituted DOM/computed-style inspection instead. Where it could inject the live detector script, it got genuine `impeccable`-prefixed console findings (not simulated) on Settings, Dashboard, and Transactions.

## Overall Impression

The app is functionally solid and internally consistent — one real backend, one component vocabulary, no dead ends — but visually it is a well-executed *generic* SaaS dashboard, not an *Onazi* one. The biggest opportunity is the same thing Product Principle #1 already names: nothing on screen right now says "built for Mozambique" the way the account types and currency handling already do under the hood. The second-biggest, unrelated to branding: a real accessibility gap (secondary text/icon gray fails contrast almost everywhere) and one real layout bug (Reports page overflows horizontally on mobile) that should get fixed regardless of which direction the rebrand takes.

## What's Working

- **Destructive-action copy names real consequences** instead of a generic "are you sure?" — e.g. account deletion warns "Todas as transações associadas serão também eliminadas," category deletion warns about associated budgets too.
- **One disciplined component system** (Button/Card/IconBadge/EmptyState/ConfirmModal) reused identically across accounts, categories, budgets, goals, transactions, and recurring pages — learn one CRUD flow, you've learned all of them.
- **Trustworthy-money signals are functionally wired, not decorative**: negative balances render danger-red from real balance math, and budget progress bars color/badge from actual 80/90/100% usage thresholds.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Generic, template-borrowed visual identity**
- What: The palette/type/icon system is a near-1:1 copy of a Notion-style productivity template, with zero Mozambique-specific visual character. Mobile-money accounts (M-Pesa/e-Mola) get no distinct color/icon treatment from bank or cash accounts.
- Why it matters: Product Principle #1 explicitly states local fit — not feature parity — is the reason to choose Onazi; the current UI contradicts that positioning visually.
- Fix: Give the account types default distinguishing presets so "Dinheiro Móvel" is legible at a glance, not just a text label. This is the core question the rebrand should answer.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable colorize` / `$impeccable shape`

**[P1] Secondary text and icon gray fails WCAG AA contrast almost everywhere**
- What: Verified directly — `--muted-foreground: #9b9a97` on white measures **2.81:1**, well under the 4.5:1 AA minimum for normal text. This token drives nav icons, form labels, and most secondary body copy across every page.
- Why it matters: A large share of the app's actual text is technically illegible to low-vision users, and it's an easy, objective thing to get right while the palette is already being revisited for the rebrand.
- Fix: Darken `--muted-foreground` (and its dark-mode counterpart) until it clears 4.5:1, or reserve it strictly for large/bold text and introduce a second, darker "secondary" tone for small body copy and labels.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable audit` / `$impeccable colorize`

**[P1] No undo after destructive delete**
- What: `ConfirmModal` fires the delete immediately on confirm — used for accounts, categories, transactions, budgets, goals, recurring — with zero recovery path, even though cascading deletes are explicitly warned about in the copy.
- Why it matters: In a product whose entire brand promise is trustworthiness with real money, one misclick permanently destroys an account's entire transaction history with no safety net.
- Fix: Give the toast system an "Undo" action with a short grace window before the delete actually commits, at minimum for account/category cascade deletes.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

**[P2] Reports page overflows horizontally on mobile**
- What: Verified mechanism — two chart containers on `/reports` render at a fixed ~441px width instead of scaling to a 375px viewport, pushing the document ~103px wider than the screen and shoving the mobile bottom nav's last item partially off-screen. The same chart component does *not* overflow on `/dashboard`, so this is a Reports-page-specific wrapping issue, not a component bug.
- Why it matters: A misclick-off-screen nav item and sideways scroll on the one page meant to show the user's financial trends is a real, concrete defect, unrelated to the rebrand.
- Fix: Give the Reports page's chart containers the same responsive width constraint the Dashboard's copy already has.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable audit`

**[P2] Transactions filter bar exceeds the working-memory limit**
- What: Search + Type + Account + Category + Period + Sort — 6 controls shown simultaneously before any results appear.
- Why it matters: Exceeds the ≤4-item cognitive-load guidance; reads as a wall of controls, especially for the common case of a small dataset.
- Fix: Default to Search + Period visible; move Type/Account/Category/Sort behind a "Filtros" disclosure.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`

**[P2] Color/icon swatch pickers are unlabeled for screen readers**
- What: `ColorPicker` and `IconPicker` (used inside the account/category/goal forms) render bare buttons with no `aria-label` — a screen reader announces "button" repeatedly with no name. (Assessment B's separate icon-only-button scan found 0 missing labels, but it only visited pages directly, not the modals these pickers live inside — so the two results aren't actually in conflict, just different coverage.)
- Why it matters: A screen-reader user cannot meaningfully customize an account, category, or goal's appearance.
- Fix: Add `aria-label` per swatch (color name/hex, icon name) and a visible "Selecionado: X" text summary.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

**[P3] Modal lacks focus management and ARIA wiring**
- What: The shared `Modal` closes on Escape/backdrop but never moves initial focus into the dialog, never restores focus to the trigger on close, and doesn't wire `aria-labelledby`/`aria-describedby`. `ProgressBar` has no `role="progressbar"`/`aria-valuenow`.
- Why it matters: Keyboard/screen-reader users get no automatic entry point into a just-opened dialog.
- Fix: Focus the first field (or close button) on open, restore focus to the trigger on close, add the ARIA attributes; add `role="progressbar"` with value attributes.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User)**: No keyboard shortcuts anywhere, not even to open "Nova transação"; no bulk select/delete on the 20-row-paginated transaction list — clearing test data means one modal confirmation per row; no CSV/export on Reports despite it being the natural power-user ask there.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)**: Color/icon pickers are entirely unlabeled, blocking screen-reader customization outright; `Modal` never places or traps initial focus, so opening any dialog via keyboard drops the user wherever focus already was; most secondary text and icons fail contrast outright (verified 2.81:1).

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)**: The Reports page's off-screen bottom-nav item (from the verified mobile overflow bug) is exactly Casey's failure mode — an important nav target partially unreachable by thumb because the page is wider than the screen.

## Minor Observations

- Native `<input type="date">` fields expose OS/browser-locale date pickers, inconsistent with the app's own enforced dd/MM/yyyy display elsewhere.
- Toasts auto-dismiss at a fixed 3s with no pause-on-hover or manual dismiss — stepping away means missing the confirmation.
- The 16-color swatch palette (bright rainbow) is unrelated to Onazi's own restrained ink+indigo system, so user-created categories/goals can visually clash with the app chrome.
- Six empty states can render simultaneously on a new user's first dashboard visit (zero balance, no transactions, no budgets, no goals, both charts empty) under the cheerful line "vamos poupar mais este mês" — a flat first impression.
- One focused element measured a white (`rgb(255,255,255)`) focus-outline color — worth a manual look, since a white outline could be low-visibility depending on what's behind it.

## Questions to Consider

1. Mobile money (M-Pesa/e-Mola) is called out as a first-class differentiator in the product brief — should those account types get their own recognizable color/mark instead of sharing the same neutral card as "Carteira"?
2. Is instant, irreversible cascade deletion (account → all its transactions, gone with one click) really the right default for a product whose entire brand promise is trustworthiness with real money?
3. If "local fit over feature parity" is principle #1, what would visibly change on screen — not just in translated copy — if this app were designed for Maputo specifically rather than reskinned from a generic productivity template?
